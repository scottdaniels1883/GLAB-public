    The lesson book

    1The most profitable way to play: what's actually possible

    Every blackjack decision has one mathematically best answer. Players who guess give the casino an edge of 2% or more. Players who follow basic strategy cut it to about 0.4% under these rules. That means an average loss of about 40 cents per $100 wagered.

    Basic strategy alone never makes blackjack profitable. Only card counting flips the edge to the player, and only in some hands. A well-played count with a 1-to-10 bet spread earns roughly 0.5% to 1.5% on money wagered. The profit comes from betting big on the few hands where you have the edge, and small everywhere else.

    The path this app teaches

    1. Learn basic strategy until your accuracy stays above 99%.
    2. Learn to keep the running count with the count hidden, using the quiz.
    3. Convert to the true count and size your bets with the ramp the coach suggests.
    4. Add the count-based plays in Settings. They are worth about another 0.1% to 0.2%.

    Why the chart looks like this

    • The dealer has no choices. They must hit until 17. Against a 2 through 6 they often end up drawing to a bust, so you stand on weak totals and let them break.
    • Against 7 through ace, assume the dealer makes 17 or better. Standing on 12 to 16 then loses too often, so you hit.
    • Double when you're ahead. Doubling puts more money down in the spots where you win more than you lose, like 11 against a 6.
    • Split to escape bad totals or to press good ones. Two 8s make 16, the worst hand. Two 8-starts are much better. Two aces make two shots at 21.
    • Surrender is the escape hatch. Giving up half your bet beats a hand that wins less than 25% of the time.
    3Counting cards with Hi-Lo

    Small cards help the dealer, who must hit stiff hands. Tens and aces help you: more blackjacks (paid 3:2), better doubles, and more dealer busts. Counting tracks which kind is left in the shoe.

    2 3 4 5 6+1A small card left the shoe, which helps you

    Running count to true count

    Add up every card you see. That's the running count. A +6 means more with one deck left than with five, so divide by the decks remaining. That gives the true count. A running count of +6 with 2 decks left is a true count of +3.

    What the true count is worth

    Each +1 of true count is worth about +0.5% to the player. With a starting edge of about −0.4%, you are roughly even at a true count of +1. You have an advantage from +2 upward.

    Practice routine

    • Hide the count in Settings and keep it in your head.
    • Count the dealer's hole card when it's turned over, not before.
    • Use the quiz before each bet. Aim for 20 correct in a row.
    • Count in pairs: a 5 and a K cancel to 0, so you can skip both.

    Bankroll and risk

    • Swings are large even with an edge. A common guideline is a bankroll of at least 500 to 1,000 times your minimum bet to keep the risk of going broke low.
    • With $1,000 here, a $10 unit is aggressive. That's intentional, so you can feel the variance quickly.
    • Never chase losses by raising bets at a negative count. The count decides the bet, not your mood.

    6Before you play for real money
    • Pick the table carefully. Never play where blackjack pays 6:5. It adds about 1.4% to the house edge, more than counting can overcome. Avoid continuous shuffling machines; they make counting useless.
    • Skip side bets. Most carry house edges of 3% to 10% or more.
    • Expect pushback. Counting in your head is legal in the US, but casinos can refuse your action or ask you to leave.
    • Variance is real. Even perfect counters have losing months. Play only with money you can afford to lose, and set a loss limit before you sit down.
    • If gambling stops being fun, help is available at 1-800-GAMBLER.
